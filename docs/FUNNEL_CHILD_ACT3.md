## Act 3 — Interfractal

You do not land on a door.

Cloudburst lets go, and the next blink is already the inside of a sky. No Light. No Whole. No glowing dot asking you to press. That gate stayed in the storm. You are in the instrument room — a late-evening blue chamber, almost black at the edges, with a table hanging in the middle of it like a map that learned how to float.

A thin bar of paper-pale chips rides the top of the dark. A round menu already says **Cloud Nine**. Open it and five names sit in a stack, like floors you can visit: Cloud Nine, Self, Tribe, World, Earth. Cloud Nine is the one that is on. Beside it, tiny letters whisper *Unearned sky · no fiber yet*. The weather you just fell through has not drawn a path home. This is the sky you fall from, still unearned.

Next to the names, an empty oval waits: *Name a goal.* A bright chip says **Set this goal**, but it sleeps until you type. **Commit** sits beside it, quiet as a stone. Those stay on the table. They are play pieces, not a shop. They do not take you to pay.

The middle is the wonder.

A gold ring lies on a faint grid, a plaza drawn on water. Nested glass worlds turn slowly, one inside another — rainbow skins, wire cages, a cube that looks like it should not fit until you tilt your head. Loops of light swim through the air: ivory, sage, one teal thread, one rose thread. They never tangle. Tiny beads ride them. Points shine like people who have become stars. The lower half of the map is a ghost of the upper half, as if the table keeps a reflection that thinks for itself.

It is a picture of points and curves. It looks like it knows everything. It does not decide for you. Walk around it. The pretty loops are evidence of a sky with rooms in it — not a Yes that speaks your name.

Drag the dark and the whole sky turns. On a small screen, six little keys wait in the corner — Q W E over A S D — so your thumbs can spin the map. **Cycle** sends the camera down the names: Cloud Nine, then Self, then Tribe, then World, then Earth, then back up through the stack. **Still** holds the weather so the loops stop racing. **Log** keeps a short diary of what the room already did. None of that is a purchase. None of that is a promise.

On the left — or tucked under the bar if the screen is narrow — a quiet card says **Close**. Two pills wait there for later, not for now.

**Checkout — later** is gray and asleep. It will not wake if you poke it. There is no pay door tonight. It is a pillow with a label, not a cash register.

**Cloud 6 — later** is bright, paper-white on the dark. That one is a door. It points toward another world — Boulder Joe’s arcade sky — for when you are done looking at the table. It does not yank you. It waits.

If you want, you can type a goal and watch the table listen. If you open Self, eight beads can wake around the cube: physical, emotional, intellectual, spiritual, environmental, social, financial, occupational. You can leave them sleeping. The loops keep turning either way.

The storm dropped you here. The table was already lit. The map keeps drawing rooms in the sky. Any Yes is still yours.
