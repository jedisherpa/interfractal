## Source-grounded model

W3C PROV-DM is the strongest conceptual anchor. It defines provenance around entities, activities, and agents, and includes derivations, responsibility, collections, and bundles. An entity can be a physical, digital, conceptual, real, or imaginary thing with an identifier and attributes; an activity acts on or with entities over time. A derivation is a transformation, update, or construction of one entity from an earlier entity. PROV-DM says that optional activity, generation, and usage details make the derivation path explicit and aid provenance analysis and reproducibility ([PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/), lines 596–626 and 851–867).

For Gate 6, a local source record, external requirement, reference definition, summary, correction, and replay/run can therefore be represented as distinct typed entities or activities. A reference-dependent summary should be a new derived entity generated by an explicit summarization activity that uses the source record entities, the reference ID/version and wording/scope/criterion, and the rule/version or hash. The summary should retain those IDs and the explicit path. This is an application design derived from PROV; PROV does not prescribe an expandable UI or claim that a derivation is correct.

PROV-DM’s bundle is useful for preserving an account of an observation or interpretation. A bundle is a named set of provenance descriptions and is itself an entity, so its provenance can also be expressed. The identifier identifies a unique set of descriptions. Gate 6 can use separate named bundles for two local views, a reference disagreement, or a correction history, preserving the prior bundle rather than overwriting it. This is provenance of provenance, not a formal fiber bundle.

PROV-O makes the relation vocabulary concrete. It provides `prov:Entity`, `prov:Activity`, and `prov:Agent`, with `prov:used`, `prov:wasGeneratedBy`, `prov:wasDerivedFrom`, `prov:wasAttributedTo`, and `prov:wasAssociatedWith`. Its expanded relations distinguish `prov:wasRevisionOf`, `prov:hadPrimarySource`, `prov:specializationOf`, and `prov:alternateOf`; collections can retain members, and bundles can retain provenance assertions. In particular, a context- or time-specific summary can be represented as a derived or specialized entity while the stable/general source record remains available. Alternative summaries can remain separate alternates or separate bundles. Adding or removing an assertion creates a distinct PROV-O bundle, which supports immutable versioning of disagreements ([PROV-O](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/), lines 314–324).

OpenLineage supplies an operational pattern rather than a semantic theory. Its object model distinguishes Jobs and Datasets and creates a lineage graph by weaving observations from many jobs. It separates runtime `RunEvent` from design-time `JobEvent` and `DatasetEvent`; events may carry job, run, input, and output details, and later run-state updates are additive. Jobs are namespace/name identified, runs have unique IDs, and datasets have namespace/name identity with static facets separated from run-specific input/output facets such as versions or partitions ([OpenLineage Object Model](https://openlineage.io/docs/spec/object-model/), version 1.53.0 observed at access).

That pattern maps cleanly to a Gate 6 record contract: stable local facts and reference/rule definitions are retained; each summarization or correction activity has a run ID; input and output IDs are explicit; source/build/rule versions are recorded; and later observations append to history. The OpenLineage parent-run guidance adds a useful recovery limit: forwarded parent facets are convenience metadata, while resolving the parent’s own event remains authoritative ([Parent Run Facet](https://openlineage.io/docs/spec/facets/run-facets/parent_run/), lines 58–62). Gate 6 should therefore treat compact outward summaries as convenience views and keep the local source records/bundles authoritative.

## Gate 2 fixture as the local test context

Gate 2 already supplies the right kind of fictional fixture. `fictional-records.json` preserves four immutable records extracted from the supplied repair-event facts: Hall, Field, Studio, and a weather/task context. Each record retains source provenance, a source-document hash, a content hash, and extraction history. The two explicit reference definitions are `R_CAPACITY_60` (“which venues hold at least 60 households?”) and `R_EXISTING_ACCESS` (“which venues already have wheelchair access without added equipment?”). The weather context remains unmapped in both views.

The recorded switch is a model for Gate 6: Hall changes from capacity yes to existing-access no because a ramp is required, while its capacity and ramp facts remain; Field stays yes; Studio changes from no to yes; and switching back restores the original mapping. These are declared rule outputs over fictional facts, not evidence of attention, influence, authority, or agreement. Gate 6 should preserve the same separation: changing a reference may change a summary or assignment, but it must not rewrite source records or invent endorsement.

## Required Gate 6 evidence and counterexamples

The sources imply a compact but strict record contract:

| Gate 6 case | Required record | What it establishes | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reference-dependent summary | Summary entity; source IDs/content hashes; reference ID/version/wording/scope/criterion; rule version/hash; producing activity/run; attribution | The outward summary can be expanded to its exact local inputs and rule | Provenance does not make the summary true or useful |
| Similar summaries, different dependencies | Two summaries with equal or near-equal text but different `used`/derivation edges | Text equality does not establish equal dependency; graph/table must expose the difference | This is a fixture-level instrument check, not a human interpretation result |
| Changed external requirement | New requirement entity/version; local use edge; correction/revision activity; corrected summary; responsible agent/plan; preserved prior bundle | The change can be traced from requirement through local record to attributable correction | “Attributable” records responsibility; it does not prove causal efficacy or legitimate authority |
| Retained disagreement | Separate reference versions or bundles with their own assertions and provenance | Conflicting accounts remain inspectable and recoverable | A graph does not resolve which account is right |
| Unjustified link | Edge type, endpoints, evidence/source IDs, rule/version, producing activity/assertion; missing fields marked unsupported | A visually tempting cross-context edge is exposed as unsupported rather than silently accepted | A missing edge is not evidence that no real-world relation exists |
| Plain comparator | Same stable records, hashes, rules, statuses, unknowns, and links available in table and graph modes | Graph emphasis does not alter the evidence set | No usability or comprehension claim without a later authorized study |

Use application relation types such as `shared-source`, `depends-on`, `contributes-to`, `revises`, and `corrects` only after defining their endpoint types, evidence requirements, and version semantics. They may be serialized as project-specific subproperties or attributes around PROV relations. A graph edge must not be inferred from proximity, common geometry, or a shared label. PROV’s broad influence relation is not a license to infer social influence, agreement, authority, trust, or commitment from a link.

## Bundle distinction and scale boundary

The Gate 6 visual idea is a connected graph of local models across scales. The appropriate description is “bundle-inspired” or “a graph of local provenance models” until a formal bundle is actually specified and checked. A formal fiber bundle would require a base space, total space, projection, fibers, and compatible local trivializations/transition structure. W3C PROV’s `prov:Bundle` instead means a named set of provenance descriptions, possibly with provenance of that set. The two concepts should not be conflated.

Likewise, organizational scale is not a new geometric dimension. A local context at one scale and a context at another scale may be linked by a typed contribution, dependency, revision, or shared-source edge while retaining their own records and histories. Those edges establish the declared data relation in the instrument. They do not establish a physical higher-dimensional space or a universal law of collective development.

## Limits

These sources support an inspectable, typed, versioned provenance/dependency design. They do not establish the Gate 6 UI, the summary wording, cross-context semantics, human usability, human comprehension, coordination benefit, authority, or truth of any fictional or real-world conclusion. An expandable summary and recoverability threshold are explicit project requirements built from provenance primitives. A source record or bundle must remain recoverable even when a summary is compact, changed, disputed, or unsupported. No Gate 7 interpretation or human participation follows from this source pass.
