The geometric conditions share model information, while differing in simultaneous access, memory demands and frame selection. Static also allocates less drawing area per observation than a single-frame view. The local-object and plain-table pair uses the same records, reference criteria and receiver changes; no extra reasoning rule is added to only one view. The plain presentation condenses the records into rows, while the local-object layout makes grouping prominent. These are layout observations, not evidence that either improves understanding.

The bounded answer to the original Looking Glass question is that a faithful local instrument can expose selected properties of declared four- and five-coordinate models and preserve inspectable, reference-dependent fictional records. Known observations can sometimes determine a property; others remain compatible with different sources. The experiment has not shown that a person can accurately interpret the unfamiliar cases, that geometry outperforms a simpler presentation, or that geometric relationships establish shared meaning, social influence or new physical dimensions.
