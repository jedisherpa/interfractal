# Shared meaning, analogy, and collective capability

Design amendment v0.3 · 20 September 2026 · Prepared for Paul Cooper

Status: the user asked to revise and carry forward the shared-meaning research question. This document integrates that direction into the existing program. Its additions have not undergone the independent review recorded for v0.2. No new agent experiment, human study, or application implementation was run for this amendment.

## Governing question

**Under what conditions, if any, can an interactive visual model help participants build, test, and revise shared meaning across perspectives and scales, improving coordination while preserving consequential differences and participant agency?**

In everyday language: can a picture help us discover what we mean, understand enough of one another to do something together, and keep the differences we still need to see?

The broader collective-capability question remains: how do distinct participants develop a sustained capability, steward it, and participate in larger arrangements? The revised question brings the visualizer's contribution and the process of shared interpretation into the foreground. A negative visualizer result does not invalidate the broader capability inquiry.

## What the question commits us to testing

| Construct | Working definition | Evidence sought |
|---|---|---|
| Shared meaning | Sufficiently compatible interpretations of a specified situation or expression for a declared task | Participants can anticipate relevant interpretations, identify boundaries, and apply the distinction to unfamiliar cases |
| Consequential difference | A difference that could change a task-relevant inference, action, obligation, limit, or correction | The difference can be found and explained before and after a shared summary is formed |
| Coordination | Participants organize complementary contributions to meet task constraints | Independently scored task outcomes, time, resource use, and response to changed requirements |
| Participant agency | Ability to author, inspect, question, and revise one's represented position and make applicable participation choices | Actual correction/withdrawal paths and outcomes; human reports where humans participate |
| Scale | A specified change in organizational or descriptive level | Individual account → group account → use of that group account in another arrangement |

Keep shared understanding, endorsement, commitment, and task success separate. A person can correctly understand a proposal and reject it. Better agreement scores alone are insufficient evidence. An AI agent's reported state is an output record, not a measurement of lived experience or human agency.

Three claims remain independently testable: participants develop usable shared meaning; this supports better coordination; offering the visualizer improves either compared with the same evidence and operations presented plainly. Do not treat a sequence of correlated improvements as proof of a causal mechanism.

## The candidate mechanism

People or agents encounter situations, recognize or propose analogies, express distinctions, compare interpretations, and revise them through feedback. A group may stabilize a reusable expression for a relationship. That expression can support a more complex shared account while the examples, exceptions, and disagreement remain recoverable.

This is a proposed account of recognition and coordination. It does not presume identical private experiences, a universal geometry of meaning, or a proven historical origin of all language. A map of an expanding, bounded collection of uses is the initial ambition. Exhaustively containing every possible meaning or experience is not an acceptance criterion.

The meaning of Paul's phrase “essence is sound” remains open: physical vibration and lived resonance are different interpretations. This amendment does not silently select either or make resolving that philosophical question a prerequisite for a bounded experiment.

## First experiment: four independent snapshots, followed by comparison

This is a proposed agent feasibility bench before the human visualizer study. It checks records, synthesis, and task-local interpretation. It does not establish that humans benefit from a picture. Preserve the v0.2 lantern bench as a separate, already executed calibration exercise.

1. **Prepare a small world with checkable relations.** Create a synthetic event set containing exploration, understanding, explicit agreement, changed requirements, and unresolved differences. Write the scoring key before agent outputs. Include different-looking cases with the same relevant relation and similar-looking cases with different relations. Human word meaning in general is not determined by this synthetic key.
2. **Distribute partly overlapping evidence.** Retain four agents for continuity and inspectability. Proposed first packet: six cards each, with two identical anchor cards and four distinct cards per agent, giving eighteen unique cards overall. This is a manageable debugging choice, unrelated to eight aspects or twelve lenses. Freeze IDs, content, allocation, prompts, model/version, settings, and token/tool budgets. Keep perspective instructions the same initially so evidence differences are not confounded with prescribed expert lenses.
3. **Freeze independent snapshots.** Agents cannot see peer outputs. Each gives its best bounded account: interpretations, relationships, source IDs, alternatives, unknowns, confidence, possible actions, and distinctions between understanding and commitment. “Complete” means all required fields and supplied evidence are accounted for, with missing knowledge declared.
4. **Compare before discussion.** Preserve the original snapshots. Align accounts using common event IDs, referents, time cutoffs, and typed relations. Produce a plain comparison and a visual proposal from the same record. Every combined claim must identify its sources and which inputs made it possible. Four repetitions of one shared card remain one source. Screen overlap is not evidence of semantic agreement.
5. **Allow a bounded exchange and freeze revisions.** Agents can offer examples, challenge interpretations, and correct their accounts. Log what changed, why, and whether a relevant dissent disappeared from the summary. Preserve both initial and revised versions.
6. **Test unfamiliar cases.** Score relational transfer, misleading-analogy rejection, distinction recovery, and coordination separately. Include a correct understanding paired with explicit rejection, and a new constraint that makes an earlier shared account inadequate. If the agents confidently unify an incorrect account, count the failure.

The concrete next deliverable is the eighteen-card fixture, withheld transfer cases, independently checked answer key, snapshot schema, and packet manifest. These are not claimed as delivered by this framing amendment. Resolve ambiguity in the task and scoring before collecting comparative results.

## Comparisons and inference

For the agent bench, compare predeclared simple pooling, a single solver receiving the full union of evidence, and a bounded interactive synthesis of the four snapshots. Account for total generation, retrieval, and synthesis cost, and report information access and compute differences. The union solver must have enough context to receive the union; resource matching must not secretly truncate its evidence. Repeated independent runs and varied task allocations are needed before performance estimates are credible. One quartet is a demonstration, not a sample supporting general superiority.

Evidence complementarity, assigned perspectives, interaction, and visual presentation are different factors. Change them separately or use an explicitly designed factorial study. Language-model agents already possess learned language; their formation of a local convention is not a reenactment of the origin of human language.

The human visualizer comparison remains the v0.2 B/C design: a capable plain workspace versus the same evidence and operational capabilities with coordinated visual views available. Shared-meaning measures are added to task development as separate outcomes. Do not silently replace the later trial's primary capability endpoint with agreement or apparent coherence. Freeze any endpoint revision, scoring rule, sample rationale, and analysis before the applicable study.

## Visualizer contract

| Element in the proposed language view | Meaning | Required inspection |
|---|---|---|
| Shared center | Current question, situation, or referent | Exact scope and version |
| Participant field | Interpretations supported by that participant's recorded account | Examples, alternatives, unknowns, and author corrections |
| Overlap | Task-specific compatibility supported by records or checks | Criterion, supporting cases, counterexamples, and uncertainty |
| Path | A sequence of recorded interpretation changes | Order, triggering input, former state, and revised state |
| Shared anchor | A reusable distinction or expression the group has established for this task | Origin, scope, exceptions, dissent, and revision history |

These are proposed semantics for a distinct language view, not descriptions of existing Interfractal measurements. Keep this layer distinguishable from views where points represent people. A camera rotation may reveal a different projection; it must not silently change evidence or infer a changed belief. Preserve directed relations and who did what to whom: similar vocabulary does not imply similar meaning.

Extend the evidence contract with snapshot ID, actor/model identity, input IDs, cutoff, interpretation ID/version, relation type and argument roles, supporting/conflicting evidence, alternatives, proposed speech act, author confirmation status, confidence provenance, revision reason, and display mapping/version. Generated similarity and participant-confirmed compatibility are different record types.

## Across scales and time

After within-group records and measures work, ask a second group to coordinate through the first group's compact account. Compare that account with full records and a naive summary. Test whether the second group detects limits and dependencies, and whether a changed external requirement reaches the affected internal participants. The group summary is useful when it preserves what the receiving task requires and exposes how to recover consequential detail.

Retain the earlier snapshot proposal's time distinctions: agent identity, evidence cutoff, aggregation window, and prediction horizon. Begin with one common cutoff. Introduce additional temporal resolutions only after the basic alignment works. Multiple windows over one source are related views, not independent corroboration. The AREXA material motivates this design question; it does not validate a language model, frequency law, or forecasting advantage.

## Source anchors and access limits

- **Hofstadter/Sander:** analogy, categorization, and revisable concepts inform the candidate mechanism. The full book was unavailable; the preceding analysis used accessible author material, including [Sander's interview](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/260288848_The_central_role_of_analogy_in_cognitive_science). Physical sound as ineffable essence is not established by that source.
- **Gärdenfors:** [The Geometry of Meaning](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262026789/the-geometry-of-meaning/) provides a closely related geometric conceptual-space research direction. The publisher's account was inspected, not the full book. It does not validate the present torus or sphere mapping.
- **Steels:** [The Talking Heads experiment](https://langsci-press.org/catalog/book/49) provides a precedent for agents developing shared vocabulary through situated language games. The publisher's account was inspected; no replication or full-book analysis was performed here.
- **Interfractal:** the preceding turn observed and sampled the first thirty seconds before source inspection. The opening rendered; the subsequent WebGL scene did not render in the available browser. Later geometry was assessed from source at `jedisherpa/interfractal`, commit `1326cc64223fc167610c2157f2c24fa33a87c93b`. Deployment/commit identity was not verified. The inspected [scene](https://github.com/jedisherpa/interfractal/blob/1326cc64223fc167610c2157f2c24fa33a87c93b/src/components/instrument/room-scene.tsx) uses authored geometric rules.
