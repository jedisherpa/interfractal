# Collective capability across scales: research charter

Version 0.1 working draft. Prepared 19 September 2026 for Paul Cooper. Independent agent analysis is part of the preparation, not human peer review or empirical validation.

## Outcome

Develop and test a representation of how distinct participants sustain a collective capability, steward it, and participate in larger arrangements. Determine when the visualizer improves accurate understanding, prediction, coordination, and correction enough to justify its complexity.

The practical product of the program is an evidence-based choice of representations and interaction rules, accompanied by a clear account of where they work, where they mislead, and what remains open. A useful result may be a hybrid of geometry, plain language, tables, and event history. Positive results for one task will not establish a universal geometry of social life.

## What the present work authorizes

Source research, independent agent analyses, mathematical specification, study planning, a proposed visualizer contract, reproducible synthetic study materials, and independent review of those materials. This package does not authorize recruitment, external communication, spending, deployment, production repository edits, or commitments on behalf of other people. Those actions can follow an applicable human decision; ordinary read-only research and package corrections continue now.

## Governing research question

Under what conditions do interacting participants develop a sustained collective capability, how is that capability stewarded, and how can it participate in larger patterns while its internal relationships remain visible and revisable?

### Linked questions

1. Formation: Which complementarities, interaction patterns, resources, and feedback produce task-relevant collective capability?
2. Recognition: How do participants and observers identify the relevant whole, parts, boundaries, and relationships? How do analogy and categorization change this recognition?
3. Maintenance: What must recur or adapt for the capability to persist under disruption?
4. Stewardship: How are actual influence, recognized authority, responsibility, representation, and correction distributed?
5. Cross-scale participation: What information, commitments, uncertainty, dissent, and dependencies must an outward description retain to coordinate effectively with other groups?
6. Visualizer contribution: What incremental benefit does a visual representation add to the same information and operational capabilities presented plainly?

## Working vocabulary

| Term | Working meaning | Observable or specification | Do not substitute |
|---|---|---|---|
| Participant | A person, or a bounded group acting through an explicit interface, in a specified task | Identity, scope, contribution, declared relations | Geometric vertex as whole-person identity |
| Lens | A perspective prompt or authored contribution; record which kind | Source, author, revision, task scope | Fixed personality or natural taxonomy |
| Shared reference | An identified purpose, question, activity, or versioned proposal | What participants say it is; areas of mismatch | Numerical centroid automatically interpreted as leader |
| Affinity | Willingness or attraction to continued association | Optional direct report plus participation behavior | Agreement or capability |
| Coherence | Compatibility sufficient for a specified joint activity over a specified interval | Task-dependent coordination conditions; retained disagreement | Universal score, unanimity, or visual symmetry |
| Capability | Ability to attain specified task outcomes under constraints | Outcome quality, time, resources, robustness | Fluent synthesis, attendance, agreement |
| Collective advantage | Improvement attributable to interaction under a defined comparison | Resource-matched independent or nominal-group baseline | Simply exceeding one under-resourced individual |
| Influence | An attributable effect on attention, decisions, or action | Causal claims require stronger design; descriptive traces first | Speaking time, centrality, or role title alone |
| Authority | Recognized permission to make a specified decision/commitment | Scope, grant source, holder, version and revocation | Model confidence or inferred influence |
| Stewardship | Practices that sustain and responsibly direct shared capability | Maintenance, dependency handling, representation, repair | Automatic ownership of the group by a leader |
| Scale | Level of organization or description selected for a task | Explicit mapping between internal and external description | Spatial dimension or superior human worth |
| Dynamic stability | Persistence/recovery of relevant coordination under change | Defined interval, perturbation and recovery criterion | Frozen equality of all relationships |
| Legibility | Accurate, usable interpretation by a named audience | Understanding, prediction, correction and effort | Attractiveness or subjective ease alone |
| Convergence | Development of a usable shared pattern while distinctions can persist | Task-defined usable result with unresolved matters recorded | Averaging, unanimous belief, software status string |

These definitions are commitments for measurement design, not claims of consensus across all disciplines. Domain-specific revisions must be logged with reasons and implications for comparability.

## Formal and empirical scope

Represent a group at time t by its participant states, typed relationships, contributions, resources, shared reference, commitments, decision rights, and attributable history. A task-specific projection produces the outward interface used at the next scale. The projection must identify what it omits and how an observer can recover relevant detail; it need not reconstruct all internal states.

There are at least three separate tests: (a) the representation is internally well-defined; (b) it preserves distinctions needed for the task; (c) using it improves human performance or understanding. Success on (a) does not imply (b) or (c).

Geometry is one candidate encoding. Graphs/hypergraphs describe relations; time describes changes; geometric dimension describes coordinates or degrees of freedom. Hopf geometry provides a precise mathematical example of a many-to-one projection with circle fibers. A social mapping still needs an independently justified state space, equivalence relation and observable interpretation.

## Preserved design choices and permitted revisions

Eight aspects and twelve archetypes are provisional. Four is a convenient first fixture because current code and scenarios support it and its six pairwise relations remain inspectable; it is not an optimal-group-size conclusion. Three and five are nearby sensitivity cases; twelve is a later coordination-load case. No first study will be powered to discover a universal group-size threshold.

Every change to count, shape, lens taxonomy, convergence criterion or shared reference must record: previous choice; proposed choice; explanatory or empirical reason; invariant preserved; cost/benefit; test or counterexample; status; and decision owner. Replacing one aesthetically attractive integer with another does not satisfy this rule.

## What would count against the model

- A purported higher-level unit has no stable task-relevant interface or predictive usefulness.
- Reported collective advantage disappears under a fair resource-matched comparison.
- The picture implies agreement, endorsement or authority that participants did not express.
- The same state produces materially different judgments merely through irrelevant geometric presentation.
- Similar-looking states with different obligations cannot be distinguished reliably.
- The geometry adds cognitive cost without commensurate task benefit.
- A chosen social-to-geometric correspondence fails its own mathematical requirements.
- A group achieves the target capability through a different structure; the program must accommodate or explain it rather than disqualifying the group.

## Delivery posture

Preserve urgency through staged decisions and a small runnable first study. The fast path establishes semantic fidelity and feasibility, not general efficacy. Continue building only where evidence gates permit; maintain a plain representation as a usable fallback. An unsuccessful visual encoding can be revised without invalidating every part of the underlying coordination question.
