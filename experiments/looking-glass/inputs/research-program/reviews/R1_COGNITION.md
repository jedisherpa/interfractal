# R1 — Analogy, categorization, and recognition of collective capability

Prepared 2026-09-19. Independent AI-generated research review; no human participants, experiments, or external expert endorsement. Current user corrections govern over earlier drafts.

**Remit.** Examine how participants recognize a situation, construct and revise representations, identify a collective as a usable unit, and transfer learning between stories, games, and practical coordination. This perspective does not determine constitutional legitimacy, prove collective consciousness, or identify a universally correct geometry. Its distinctive question is whether the visualizer helps people notice consequential relationships that they otherwise miss, while remaining able to discover that their current interpretation is wrong.

**Access boundary.** The complete text of *Surfaces and Essences* was not accessible in this review. The publisher description and Sander's complete six-page, author-uploaded interview were accessible. The local full-text extraction of Chalmers, French, and Hofstadter's March 1991 technical report was available; the introduction, representation critique, and Copycat discussion were inspected. Related original studies and their access limits are recorded below. No claim here is based on having read the complete book.

## Seven findings

**1. FACT — The authors' position is broader, and less anti-surface, than a slogan about looking beneath appearances.** Sander describes analogy as connecting present situations with earlier experiences, supporting concept formation, retrieval, inference, and recategorization. Categories change through use rather than functioning solely as fixed boxes. He also argues that salient surfaces often provide useful access to abstractions; learning must subsequently support recognition across different surfaces. These are the author's theoretical claims, not settled findings about every cognitive process. His caution that recategorization is constrained by prior knowledge also rules out a recipe in which merely adding viewpoints guarantees creativity. [Sander and Bianchini, 2013](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/260288848_The_central_role_of_analogy_in_cognitive_science).

**DESIGN implication:** Preserve the pictures' explanatory ambition. A ring may provide a memorable cue for maintained commitments. Test whether learners can subsequently recognize maintenance, dependence, or revision without the ring, and avoid inferring these relations whenever a ring appears. “Essence” should mean a proposed task-relevant relationship, open to revision; it should not become a hidden natural substance supposedly shared by all collectives.

**2. FACT — Representation formation is itself part of the problem.** Chalmers, French, and Hofstadter criticize analogy systems whose researchers preselect the relevant objects and relations. They broadly accept the value of preserving relational structure while disputing whether matching supplied representations captures the difficult perceptual work. Their Copycat account allows context, tentative groupings, and analogical mappings to influence one another. It is a computational account in a restricted letter-string domain, not an experiment showing that teams should behave like cooperating codelets. Its eventual selection of a representation is not a social requirement for unanimity. [Technical report, §§1–4](https://consc.net/papers/highlevel.pdf); inspected through the supplied local extraction.

**DESIGN implication:** A visualizer should expose where its categories came from. Record the original statement, participant's grouping, system-suggested grouping, and subsequent changes separately. If a synthesis “discovers” a collective only after the interface has required everyone to complete predetermined slots, the slots may have supplied the result. Permit alternative decompositions, unmapped material, overlapping membership, and an explicit “this representation misses something” contribution.

**3. USER INTENT — Recognizing a whole must remain distinguishable from everyone describing it alike.** Paul's desired whole is a sustained capability formed through differentiated participation, capable of participating in a larger arrangement. Recognition can therefore be practical: participants anticipate dependencies, know when a joint response is available, and successfully use it. They need not share an exhaustive description of their organization or possess complete individual coherence beforehand.

**HYPOTHESIS:** A shared outward representation helps coordination when members can still recover the distinctions relevant to the next task. Assess three separate outcomes: recognizing that a joint capability exists; accurately predicting what it can accomplish; and locating which internal contribution or dependency must change after feedback. A collective label alone satisfies none of these. A temporary group can have a useful capacity; persistence is a separate longitudinal outcome.

**4. FACT — Perceived group unity already has an empirical research tradition, but it is not measured capability.** Lickel and colleagues explicitly study subjective entitativity: the perception that people form a coherent unit. Their three studies examine group-property ratings and classifications. Perceived interaction, shared goals and outcomes, similarity, and importance were strongly interrelated and associated with entitativity. These correlational results do not show that any particular visual arrangement produces capability, nor that a perceived entity has a single mind. [Lickel et al., 2000, pp. 223–246](https://www.academia.edu/15348317/Varieties_of_groups_and_the_perception_of_group_entitativity).

**DESIGN implication:** Measure perceived unity and demonstrated coordination separately. Also distinguish affinity, operational dependence, influence, and recognized authority. The observer who finds a symmetric sphere convincing may be making a plausible visual grouping while making an incorrect organizational inference. Conversely, an untidy representation may depict reliable coordination. A center can stand for a reference point, bottleneck, influential contributor, or authorized leader; these require different evidence.

**5. HYPOTHESIS — Assigned lenses and self-authored perspectives offer different cognitive affordances.** A prescribed Logician or similar prompt may help a novice notice a neglected consideration. It may also make unprompted considerations harder to express, encourage role performance, or make other participants overattribute expertise. Self-authorship may reveal task-specific knowledge and permit recategorization, yet impose a larger articulation burden and yield descriptions that collaborators cannot interpret. Neither advantage is established for this system.

Compare prescribed prompts, participant-authored descriptions, and a hybrid in which participants can edit or reject suggestions. Hold information, time, facilitator attention, and ability to speak constant. Measure useful information discovered, omitted dependencies, unexpected but relevant distinctions, misattribution of expertise, and the time needed for someone else to understand the contribution. Retain both the author's intended meaning and an interlocutor's interpretation; disagreement between them is valuable data. Multiple lenses per person and role revision should remain possible in every condition so the test does not confuse lens content with restrictive participation rules.

**6. FACT — Comparing examples can support transfer, but remembering a lesson is not acting on it.** Gentner, Loewenstein, and Thompson report three experiments on learning negotiation principles. Comparing cases outperformed studying them separately on important learning and transfer outcomes; guided comparison also supported transfer in a simulated face-to-face negotiation. Their pilot found that participants could learn from training yet rarely use its principles during negotiation. Their results concern particular instructional conditions and negotiation tasks, not general team emergence or the truth of Hofstadter and Sander's entire theory. [Gentner et al., 2003, pp. 393–408](https://groups.psych.northwestern.edu/gentner/papers/GentnerLoewensteinThompson03.pdf).

**DESIGN implication:** The supplied outline already offers a useful progression. “Three Drawings, One Dragon” teaches complementary information; “The Wings That Wouldn't Fit” makes dependency consequential; “Two Good Suggestions” separates consideration from obedience. The guide openings connect exploration, commitment, and revisiting with ordinary situations and explicitly limit the geometry's theoretical status. Turn the proposed transfer activities into tests: first a new story, then a game with changed appearance, then a bounded practical task. Remove the wizard and geometric vocabulary in later probes. Ask what should happen next and why before offering the familiar lesson label.

**7. DESIGN — Cognitive framing adds information and adaptation outcomes beyond a consent audit.** A flawless permission record does not reveal whether participants noticed the wrong goal, misread a dependency, knew whom to consult, or recognized an unexpected opportunity. Nor does agreement establish that a shared representation is informative. The visualizer should support participants in constructing and comparing accounts, testing predictions, and revising the account after consequences become visible.

Allow a participant to say both “our arrangement can now do this” and “your picture of my contribution is incomplete.” Make an outward collective expression inspectable back to its supporting contributions and exceptions. Show proposed analogies with their correspondences and their breaking points. A facilitation claim such as “this resembles the dragon's connector problem” should invite examination of the dependence, rather than settle it through narrative familiarity. AI can propose these comparisons; humans determine what represents their stance and what they undertake.

## Strongest counterexample

A group learns every geometric term, experiences strong unity, and produces a polished shared account. Its task succeeds because one expert supplies all decisive information. When that expert leaves, the group cannot adapt; other participants cannot recover the reasons behind its outward statement. A second group uses ordinary notes, disagrees visibly, and maintains complementary expertise that survives a changed requirement. This would defeat the inference from visual coherence, vocabulary mastery, or a compelling collective narrative to sustained collective capability. It also challenges any study that scores only familiarity with the visualizer. Conversely, if assigned lenses outperform self-authorship under fair comparisons, the program must accept useful scaffolding rather than explain away the result as insufficient openness.

## Testable visualizer hypotheses and measures

All rows below are **HYPOTHESIS / DESIGN**, not established effects.

| Proposed mechanism | Operational observation | Result counting against it |
|---|---|---|
| Comparing representations improves relational recognition | Accuracy on novel dependency cases and rejection of visually similar cases with different dependencies | Familiar-image accuracy rises but novel-case discrimination does not |
| Recoverable internals improve useful recognition of a whole | Correct capability forecast, identification of the affected contributor, and retrieval of the relevant minority fact | Unity ratings rise while these measures fall |
| Editable lenses improve information discovery | Independently coded, evidence-relevant distinctions absent from initial prompts; interlocutor comprehension | More prose and categories without better prediction or action |
| Geometry aids learning without creating false certainty | Confidence calibrated against case accuracy; unsupported leader/consensus/capability attribution under rendering swaps | Same record acquires higher confidence or authority merely through its shape |
| Recategorization supports adaptation | Appropriate change of grouping after a task-relevant perturbation, with reasons tied to new evidence | Constant regrouping, or persistence despite disconfirming evidence |
| A collective can participate usefully in a larger arrangement | Correct cross-group dependency prediction and repair after external requirements change | Outward coordination hides internal failures or cannot transmit corrective feedback |

For case recognition report hits and false positives separately, not just agreement with a facilitator. For live tasks score task quality, recovery time, and information retained alongside subjective understanding. Preserve individual distributions: a shared representation understood only by its author is a different outcome from broadly usable coordination.

## Proposed first study and adversarial examples

**First proof obligation, before recruitment:** Construct a twelve-item recognition battery from three target relationships crossed with four combinations of similar/different appearance and preserved/changed relation. The relationships are complementary information, cross-part dependency, and collective output with recoverable internal differences. Provide source records and an answer rationale for each; include “insufficient information” when warranted. This tests the instrument, not participants, and does not require deployed software.

Matched adversarial pairs should include:

- **Same sphere, changed capability:** identical participants and layout; one record contains complementary evidence and functioning handoffs, the other repeats a single source. Ask what either group can actually infer.
- **Different surface, same dependency:** widening dragon wings breaks a connector; changing a shared data format breaks another team's import. Ask which relationship matters and where the analogy stops.
- **Same convergence appearance, changed meaning:** synchronized animation accompanies independent opinions in one case and an enacted coordination plan in another. Ask what is supported, what remains unknown, and which next observation would discriminate.
- **Same central position, changed authority:** the central node is a shared objective in one case and a person with a documented decision role in another. Then move the authorized person off-center without changing the record.

After instrument review, the minimal human pilot compares guided side-by-side case comparison with equally timed separate-case study. Both conditions receive the same examples, facts, practice opportunities, and interface polish. Administer uncued novel cases, an opportunity to act in a bounded coordination task, and a delayed probe. Run geometry versus plain-map rendering separately; mixing instructional and rendering changes would obscure the explanation. Evaluate assigned versus self-authored lenses only after the basic measures work.

A later capability study needs interacting groups, an appropriate isolated-person baseline, and a pooled-information baseline. Otherwise simple access to more information can masquerade as a distinctive interaction benefit. Change an external requirement after an initially successful round; assess whether participants revise internal dependencies and maintain the outward service. Repeated rounds can show adaptation, but sustained capability requires follow-up over an explicitly stated interval.

## Prerequisites, gates, numbers, and unresolved questions

**Prerequisites:** Paul reviews construct definitions; the team separates illustrative cases from operating rules; records support every scored inference; alternative reasonable interpretations are retained. Test materials must distinguish authority from influence and contain both useful and misleading analogies. Human recruitment, including any work with children, remains outside this review's authorization.

**Stop/revise:** Independent item reviewers cannot agree on what the evidence licenses; answers depend on remembering private vocabulary; rendering changes factual records; participants cannot express missing distinctions; or the intervention improves confidence while impairing warranted inference. Revise a confusing metaphor rather than attribute failure to participants.

**Advance:** The instrument distinguishes relational understanding from surface familiarity, and participants can use and correct the representation. A pilot estimates feasibility and variation only. Before a confirmatory study, specify primary outcome, practically meaningful improvement, tolerable error increase, sample-size rationale, and analysis of repeated/team-dependent observations. Advance practical adoption only on transfer and recovery evidence commensurate with the intended claim.

**Numbers:** Retain eight self-aspects and twelve prescribed lenses as optional historical conditions, not cognitive universals. The twelve-item battery is a factorial design choice unrelated to lens count. Retain 3, 4, and 5 as manageable group-size comparisons; 12 tests a larger coordination burden, not a ceiling. Do not change these numbers merely to obtain prettier geometry. Story ages 7–10 and short episode lengths remain editorial proposals, not experimentally established optima. No participant sample size is justified by the cited studies alone.

**Unresolved:** Whether richer representations help novices more than experienced collaborators; how cultural familiarity changes analogy retrieval; whether apparent understanding survives stress and delay; whether self-authorship distributes articulation costs unequally; and whether gains come from visualization, comparative instruction, extra attention, or improved records. Existing evidence does not adjudicate whether the collective experiences a unified mind.
