# R1 — Collective capability, coordination, stewardship, and power

Date: 19 September 2026. Status: independent AI research review for Paul Cooper; no human participants, external endorsement, or empirical validation. This review read the frozen shared brief and the supplied *Collective Capability and Participant Agency* draft, including its correction, delegation, and counterexample sections. It did not read other R1 memos.

## Remit and distinctions

The remit is to identify mechanisms by which differentiated contributors accomplish work together, maintain that capability, and coordinate with other groups. It includes who sets priorities, whose knowledge changes decisions, and who can commit resources. It does not adjudicate consciousness, derive institutions from geometry, or assume that maximizing performance supplies political legitimacy.

The following are operational distinctions proposed for this program, not a settled universal taxonomy:

| Construct | What to observe | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Affinity | Willingness to associate; interpersonal attraction; perceived common purpose | Accurate coordination or competent action |
| Functional coherence | Compatible expectations, intelligible dependencies, predictable handoffs and recoverable disagreement | Identical beliefs or absence of conflict |
| Capability | Repeated accomplishment of specified tasks under stated constraints, including perturbation | A general mind, efficiency, or legitimacy |
| Shared reference | A map, vocabulary, plan, or record participants can jointly inspect | An authorized decision |
| Actual influence | Whose contributions alter options, priorities, allocations, and action | Expertise, fair access, or a formal mandate |
| Recognized authority | Explicitly acknowledged scope to decide or commit particular resources | Effective influence or morally justified power |
| Stewardship | Maintaining resources, interfaces, learning, succession, and accountable decisions | Permanent ownership by a founder or facilitator |

## Eight substantive findings

### 1. USER INTENT / DESIGN — Formation is relational and revisable

Paul's intended convergence is a usable pattern that retains differences. A person need only become sufficiently coherent for a particular relationship; participation can help create that coherence. Accordingly, measure whether collaborators can understand an offer, predict its dependencies, and renegotiate it. Do not require an allegedly complete or internally unified person before entry. The group's outward expression may be singular while internal reasoning remains plural. Treat formation, maintenance, and revision as potentially overlapping activities rather than compulsory developmental stages. These commitments follow the current brief and supplied draft, not laboratory findings.

### 2. FACT / DESIGN — Capability above one person is meaningful but insufficient to establish an interaction advantage

Laughlin and colleagues found that groups of three, four, and five solved letters-to-numbers problems in fewer trials than the best of an equivalent number of independently working people; those group sizes did not differ on trials to solution. This is a task-specific result with a stronger comparison than an average solo participant. It supplies no general social optimum. [Laughlin et al., 2006](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/7131264_Groups_Perform_Better_Than_the_Best_Individuals_on_Letters-to-Numbers_Problems_Effects_of_Group_Size)

For this program, distinguish three claims: **expanded capability**, relative to one participant's situated resources; **interaction gain**, relative to an appropriate aggregation of equally resourced independent work; and **institutional benefit**, relative to a competent alternative coordination arrangement. A team can satisfy the first through simultaneous labor or pooled information without satisfying the second. Match information, tools, experience, and aggregate work where relevant, and report both elapsed time and person-time. These budgets cannot always be matched simultaneously; state which practical question each comparison answers. For an AI comparison, also account for inference, memory, and review budgets.

### 3. FACT / UNRESOLVED — Small groups are useful experiments, not natural ceilings

Woolley and colleagues studied 699 people in groups of two to five and reported a factor associated with performance across their task battery. Social sensitivity and conversational turn distribution correlated with that factor; the study does not establish that forcing equal speaking time causes capability, nor that cohesion is irrelevant everywhere. Its short-session results do not establish long-term institutional agency. [Woolley et al., 2010](https://chabris.com/Woolley2010a.pdf)

A different experiment tested 47 teams with nominal sizes from one to 32 on crisis mapping. Larger teams' coordination gains could outweigh reduced individual effort; the largest teams surpassed the study's synthetic independent-worker comparison. This directly challenges treating a small-group result as a general ceiling, while leaving task and aggregation dependence intact. [Mao et al., 2016](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0153048)

The number of potential pairwise connections, n(n−1)/2, is a combinatorial fact, not a measurement of required communication. Specialization, asynchronous records, and selective contact can change the realized coordination burden. Neither flatness nor hierarchy follows from group size alone.

### 4. FACT / HYPOTHESIS — Complementary knowledge can be coordinated without making everyone know everything

Liang, Moreland, and Argote compared training together with individual training followed by work in new groups. Groups trained together later recalled more of a radio assembly procedure and built better radios; the authors attributed much of the benefit to transactive memory. The comparison also changes shared working history, so it is not an isolated test of a directory interface. [Liang et al., 1995](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/240024925_Group_Versus_Individual_Training_and_Group_Performance_The_Mediating_Role_of_Transactive_Memory)

A testable mechanism here is accurate knowledge of who can answer which question, combined with timely access and revisable task ownership. A shared reference should expose dependencies and routes to expertise while retaining specialist differences. Measure actual routing accuracy and handoff errors; agreement that “we understand each other” is insufficient. Train and measure how members update their expectations after an expert becomes unavailable or a responsibility changes. Improved participant understanding through working together would support the relational formation hypothesis without proving a universal sequence of scales.

### 5. FACT / DESIGN — Leadership can be distributed while appointed leadership remains useful

Carson, Tesluk, and Marrone studied 59 MBA consulting teams. A network measure of shared leadership predicted client-rated performance; internal conditions including voice, support, and shared purpose, together with external coaching, predicted shared leadership. This observational study supports examining leadership relationships and coaching together. It does not establish that abolishing a designated leader improves every team. [Carson et al., 2007](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/275859545_Shared_Leadership_in_Teams_An_Investigation_of_Antecedent_Conditions_and_Performance)

Make stewardship concrete: someone maintains a usable purpose, checks resource adequacy, notices neglected dependencies, solicits relevant expertise, resolves or routes disputes, monitors outcomes, and prepares succession. These functions can be distributed by domain, phase, or competence. A steward needs an explicit remit, review date, substitute, and route for challenge. Leadership selection and renewal should be visible decisions. Emergence describes how influence arose; it does not itself authorize commitments.

### 6. FACT / DESIGN — Perceived leadership must be checked against actual contribution and power

In two studies, Anderson and Kilduff found that trait dominance predicted perceived competence beyond measured task ability, with competence-signaling behavior contributing to that perception. Leadership recognition therefore cannot be treated as a transparent assay of expertise. [Anderson and Kilduff, 2009](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/23807728_Why_Do_Dominant_Personalities_Attain_Influence_in_Face-to-Face_Groups_The_Competence-Signaling_Effects_of_Trait_Dominance)

Measure separate networks for seeking advice, changing decisions, controlling scarce resources, and authorizing action. Speaking time, network centrality, and a visually central position cannot substitute for these measures. Compare private initial recommendations with subsequent proposals and enacted allocations, recording source attribution and rejected contributions. Such traces indicate influence; causal attribution requires stronger designs, such as randomizing presentation order or temporarily assigning decision roles. Inspect agenda setting, control of the summarizer, and access to records as power mechanisms. A formally flat group can conceal these asymmetries; a named coordinator can make them more inspectable.

### 7. FACT / HYPOTHESIS / DESIGN — Correction and autonomy concern usable participation, not compulsory unanimity

Edmondson's study of 51 manufacturing teams associated psychological safety with learning behavior. It motivates examining interpersonal conditions for speaking up, but does not demonstrate that a software correction channel, lens system, or geometry causes learning. [Edmondson, 1999](https://web.mit.edu/curhan/www/docs/Articles/15341_Readings/Organizational_Learning_and_Change/Edmondson_1999_Psychological_safety.pdf)

Hypothesis: persistent, attributable correction improves adaptation when important evidence is distributed and errors or constraints change. Normative commitment: participants retain standing even when that intervention has no productivity benefit. Distinguish correcting attribution, disputing a factual claim, appealing a decision, revising a role, and declining a new commitment. Each needs an appropriate disposition; an objection need not win. Permit bounded delegation and time-limited decisions under an agreed rule, with recorded reasons and escalation. A temporary stop should have a defined trigger and decision owner. Otherwise unrestricted reopening can destroy dependable cooperation while merely relocating power to whoever obstructs most effectively.

### 8. FACT / DESIGN — Group-to-group participation needs its own interface

Davison and colleagues examined coordinated action in 233 multiteam systems. Relationships between coordination and performance depended on where coordination occurred and which component was central to the task; direct cross-component coordination was not uniformly helpful. The study concerns a specific military simulation and does not prove hierarchy inevitable or broadly legitimate. [Davison et al., 2012](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/51925528_Coordinated_Action_in_Multiteam_Systems)

A participating group should export a capability offer, dependencies, delivery limits, authorized representative, commitment scope and expiry, and uncertainty relevant to the receiving group. Its outward statement should link to the internal decision, relevant qualifications, and correction contact. Inward feedback needs an owner, deadline, and visible disposition. A representative may negotiate within scope; a request exceeding that scope returns for internal decision. Larger-scale membership should not silently transfer authority over every member. Measure whether feedback actually revises internal work, and whether partners can rely on accepted commitments without inspecting every private deliberation.

## Strongest counterexample and practical consequences

**Constructed counterexample:** two participants hold independent bits. A central collector computes their parity perfectly; either isolated participant can do no better than chance. No deliberation, affinity, shared leadership, or correction right is necessary on the truthful-input benchmark. A solo actor receiving both bits also computes it perfectly. This both validates the draft's situated capability gain and defeats stronger inferences from that gain to interaction synergy or participant agency. A correction interface must face cases involving distorted reports or changed conditions to show its instrumental benefit.

A second constructed case has a known correct plan, reliable specialists, a short deadline, and costly renegotiation. Bounded command can beat repeated collective reconsideration. Conversely, an opaque commander may perform poorly when indispensable corrective information sits with a low-status participant. The research must include both contexts; otherwise it selects tasks that guarantee its preferred answer.

For the visualizer, offer switchable views of shared references, dependencies, influence, and authority. Let participants contest their attributed contribution, inspect what changed, and trace a group-level claim back to an internal decision. A single glowing center would conceal precisely the distinctions at issue. Do not infer authority from a position, capability from geometric closure, or consensus from a polished summary.

## Proposed first study, prerequisites, and gates

**DESIGN:** use a low-stakes event-planning simulation with objectively distributed constraints, an executable allocation, and a later disruption. Begin with four-person teams for practical continuity with the current prototype, not because four is optimal. Establish task feasibility and baseline performance before manipulating geometry or group size.

First benchmark the capability claim against solo work with pooled information and against a prespecified competent aggregation of four independent contributions. Use both elapsed-time and person-time reporting; charge aggregation and facilitation costs. An unrealistically poor union of independent answers is not an adequate competitor.

Then randomize teams between ordinary competent facilitation and the same facilitation plus a persistent, source-linked correction channel with required disposition. Hold decision authority, information, task time, training exposure, AI access, and presentation quality constant. Preserve basic withdrawal and ordinary opportunities to raise concerns in both conditions. Test the package before attributing effects to its individual features. Keep role revision and goal reconsideration for subsequent studies.

Primary outcomes: independently scored plan quality after disruption, consequential errors, recovery time, and total effort. Mechanism outcomes: valid corrective evidence reaching a decision, source attribution accuracy, and whether action changes appropriately. Participant outcomes: ability to identify commitments, correction delay and burden, private understanding of authority, and differences by role/status. Record unwarranted reopening alongside missed corrections. A neatly completed ledger is not the task answer key.

Prerequisites are a fixed task rubric, versioned protocol, usable baseline, reliable event capture, assessor blinding where feasible, and an analysis accounting for team dependence. The pilot estimates feasibility and variation; it does not prove efficacy. Preregister meaningful effects, burden limits, exclusions, and confirmatory sample-size rationale before that phase.

Stop or repair if participants cannot distinguish proposals from commitments, source corrections overwrite contrary evidence, scoring leaks condition, or burden falls systematically on less influential members. Advance to a confirmatory comparison only after these implementation risks are resolved. Advance to multiteam work only after capability and usable delegation are demonstrated locally. A later 12-person comparison can examine one group versus three groups of four with equivalent total resources and task dependencies; it must test the interface, not presume that nesting is better.

## Numbers, uncertainties, and source-access record

Retain 3, 4, and 5 as small-model comparisons; retain 12 as a larger comparison. Add solo and nominal-group baselines because they identify different capability claims. Larger sizes remain admissible. The eight aspects of self and twelve lens labels have no evidential role in this review; no change to their content is proposed. Sample size is unresolved pending a defensible effect and variance estimate.
