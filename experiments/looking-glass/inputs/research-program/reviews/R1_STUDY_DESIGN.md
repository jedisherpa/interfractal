# R1 — Experimental design, measurement, and causal inference

**Status:** independent design memo; 19 September 2026. No participants recruited, human observations collected, protocol registered, or causal effects established. This memo uses the frozen shared brief and methods sources, without reading other R1 reviews. Its remit is to turn the proposed collective capability into falsifiable comparisons while keeping task achievement, agency, and representation distinct.

## Seven findings

1. **USER INTENT — Capability is the target, not unanimity.** A team can execute a sound plan despite disagreement about its desirability. Therefore agreement, visual symmetry, proximity to a geometric center, and apparent coherence must not define success. Task performance must be scored using constraints written before participants respond; internal differences need a separate recovery measure.

2. **HYPOTHESIS — Coordination and visualization can have different effects.** A structured explore–commit–revisit process might improve revision without a geometric interface. Conversely, a geometric view might improve retrieval while distracting from execution. A bundled intervention versus ordinary conversation cannot identify which component matters.

3. **DESIGN — Use a factorial comparison with one primary contrast.** Cross protocol present/absent with geometric/table presentation. The primary question is the added effect of geometric presentation when the protocol is already present. Protocol effects and interaction are secondary unless separately powered and multiplicity controlled. Failure to detect interaction is not evidence that the factors act independently.

4. **FACT — A pilot principally establishes feasibility.** The CONSORT pilot extension distinguishes feasibility objectives from effectiveness testing, recommends prespecified progression criteria, and cautions that small pilots provide imprecise estimates. Accordingly, this program will not advance because a pilot treatment comparison has a favorable p-value. [Eldridge et al., 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5076380/)

5. **DESIGN — More records are not more independent groups.** Randomize intact teams; repeated tasks and participants are nested observations. Four people producing many clicks do not constitute many independent treatment assignments. Learning, facilitator effects, pre-existing relationships, and withdrawal can each change the apparent treatment effect.

6. **HYPOTHESIS — Exceeding an individual requires a demanding comparator.** Beating an individual with less information or fewer person-minutes demonstrates an advantage of resources, not a distinctive coordination mechanism. The program needs an all-information, resource-matched nominal-team benchmark alongside its interface comparison.

7. **UNRESOLVED — Short-task performance is not sustained collective capability.** A successful session cannot establish stewardship, durable identity, cross-scale participation, or general emergence. Those require repeated external demands, actual revision, and observable effects of a larger arrangement on internal coordination.

## First proof obligation: protocol and measurement bench

Complete this stage even if the deadline arrives before human research becomes possible. Freeze a state schema covering individual contribution, attributed source, proposal version, expressed stance, recognized authority, commitment, revision, and outward representation. Keep shared reference, influence, and authority as different fields. A representation may link them; it must not silently equate them.

Construct scripted traces containing disagreement, a knowledgeable minority, changed evidence, delegated authority, withdrawal, conflicting commitments, and an absent participant. Replay identical traces through the geometric and table views. Verify that each fact, contributor, revision, dissent, and commitment can be recovered in both, that stale commitments are visibly stale, and that an agent cannot create a human attestation. Score against a manually checked answer key. Simulations test these invariants and the scoring pipeline; they provide no human evidence.

**Advance gate:** every consequential state transition has a reproducible test; both interfaces expose identical underlying records and action permissions; no unresolved loss or misattribution of a commitment exists. Repair and repeat failed cases. A visually persuasive demo that fails these requirements stays a teaching prototype.

## Experimental conditions and matching

| Condition | Coordination instructions | Presentation |
|---|---|---|
| P0V0 | Neutral timed work periods | Searchable table/cards |
| P0V1 | Same neutral timed work periods | Geometric view |
| P1V0 | Explore, propose commitments, review and revise | Searchable table/cards |
| P1V1 | Same structured protocol | Geometric view |

All conditions receive the same task information, release schedule, discussion channel, device class, facilitator script, total work time, and training duration. They have identical capacities to author, inspect, filter, revise, dissent, record authority, and attest. P1 changes the prompts and prescribed sequence, not access to crucial evidence or the ability to revise. P0 receives neutral attention prompts at the same times. All conditions encounter the same external changes and deadlines. This estimates the added value of protocol guidance over a capable common workspace, not over a deliberately impoverished control.

Match tutorials and practice on functional objectives, including retrieval and correction; record training completion and residual proficiency. Give both interfaces comparably finished styling and neutral names. Measure prior familiarity and expected usefulness before treatment tasks. Post-assignment proficiency or expectations are not baseline covariates for the primary adjustment. Novelty cannot be perfectly equalized; sustained-session measurement can reveal whether an early preference fades. Separate a later metaphor-framing experiment from this one: initially omit claims that a sphere signifies harmony or that a center signifies leadership. Keep prescribed lenses out of the first causal study; participants author their own contributions. Fixed versus self-authored lenses deserve a subsequent manipulation.

## A small executable task and scoring key

Use a fictional neighborhood repair event, explicitly unrelated to participants’ employment or personal disputes. The public brief requires capacity for **60 households**, wheelchair access, power, and expenditure at most **8 tokens**. Among feasible bundles choose the least expensive. Venue hire: Hall 4, Field 3, Studio 5. There are no unlisted equipment choices, split venues, or transfers.

Four private cards supply complementary facts:

| Card | Facts |
|---|---|
| Capacity | Hall 60; Field 80; Studio 50 households |
| Access | Hall requires a portable ramp costing 2; Field and Studio already accessible |
| Power | Hall and Studio have power; Field requires a generator costing 3 |
| Weather | Rain is certain; Field requires shelter costing 3; indoor venues require none |

Participants submit a venue and equipment bundle. **Initial key:** Hall plus ramp costs 6; reserve 2. Field with generator and shelter costs 9 and breaches the budget; Studio lacks capacity. Then release an identical update in every condition: Hall is unavailable, attendance falls to 45, and dry weather removes the shelter requirement. **Revision key:** Studio, no equipment, costs 5; reserve 3. Field plus generator is feasible at 6 but is not cheapest.

Score each submission out of 10: available venue with sufficient capacity, access, power, and actual cost within budget each earn 2; minimum cost among feasible bundles earns 2 only if every constraint is satisfied. Nonexistent or unavailable venues earn zero. Score initial and revised submissions separately; total 0–20. Incorrect claimed arithmetic cannot make an infeasible bundle feasible. Omitted submissions by a still-participating team at deadline score zero. Attestation, agreement, minority retention, and elegance receive no task-performance points.

This is a **bench and comprehension fixture**, likely too easy for an efficacy trial. Before that trial create parallel tasks with independently enumerated solutions, resource trade-offs, and different change types. Pilot task difficulty and scoring reliability. Root should create the public brief, four cards, timed update, response form, immutable scoring table, and two practice examples; a later task-development pass must produce a frozen scored battery. Do not present this fixture as a validated capability instrument.

## Adult usability and measurement pilot

After institutional or other appropriate research review and explicit authorization, use consenting adults outside dependent employment or educational relationships. First conduct individual cognitive interviews to check interpretations of “commit,” “authority,” “dissent,” and survey questions. Then use a small set of intact teams across the four conditions to inspect delivery and scoring. Plan the number around coverage of each condition, accessibility problems, session completion, and precision needed for feasibility decisions; no universal pilot sample size is justified here. A planning envelope of **8–12 teams of four**, explicitly provisional, could expose practical failures but cannot establish effectiveness or estimate a reliable interaction.

Proposed operational thresholds to freeze before piloting: at least 90% of scheduled task submissions technically recoverable and at least 80% of participants completing the core tutorial without live rescue; every critical attribution defect must be resolved. Report counts, denominators, intervals, reasons, and condition differences; crossing a percentage threshold in a tiny pilot is insufficient assurance. Qualitative evidence can trigger redesign despite apparently favorable completion rates. Re-pilot materially changed interfaces with new participants. Avoid carrying trained pilot participants into the main trial.

## Controlled study: estimand, analysis, and decisions

**Population:** eligible adult, initially four-person teams recruited under a defined familiarity policy. **Primary endpoint:** team mean 0–20 task-performance score across a frozen battery of novel initial-and-revision tasks, under fixed time limits. **Primary estimand:** the mean difference P1V1 minus P1V0 among randomized teams under their assigned offer, including ordinary nonadherence. Positive values favor geometry. Scope any result to this population, task battery, and versioned intervention. Explicitly defining population, treatment, endpoint, post-assignment events, and summary follows the estimand discipline of [ICH E9(R1), sections A.3–A.5](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/E9-R1_Step4_Guideline_2019_1203.pdf); its clinical context is not a claim that this is a clinical trial.

Secondary outcomes remain separate: revision success and latency; accurate recovery of another participant’s differing view; individual understanding; and agency. Measure agency through private reports of ability to contribute, correct attribution, disagree, and decline commitment, plus observed opportunities to do so and false-attestation events. Treat this questionnaire as a new measure requiring cognitive testing, not a validated scale. Record influence as proposal uptake or decision changes; record leadership and recognized authority separately. Equal speaking time is not assumed optimal. Do not convert all outcomes into a “convergence” index.

Randomize teams after baseline measures and concealed assignment generation, stratifying sparingly on familiarity and baseline task skill. Keep condition between teams to avoid protocol learning contaminating crossover comparisons. Counterbalance task variants and order within condition. Train facilitators with scripts; distribute facilitators across conditions. Mask outcome scorers to interface assignment using plain exports. Analyze team means with the prespecified primary contrast and compatible uncertainty intervals; use task-level models only with team dependence and task/order effects represented. Participant agency analyses likewise require team-aware uncertainty.

Choose a smallest worthwhile task difference **δ** through stakeholder judgment about saved errors and time before seeing comparative outcomes. Choose a separately justified maximum tolerable agency decrement **h**. Neither is available from current evidence. Sample planning must simulate the proposed contrast using plausible between-team variance, within-team task correlation, ceiling effects, attrition, and any required agency precision. Report the number of independent teams and persons separately. Use a range of variance assumptions; do not plug an unstable pilot effect into a power calculation. If interaction or subgroup claims are desired, plan their precision explicitly. If funds cannot support the required teams, retain an estimation/feasibility designation.

Proposed decision rule: advance the visualizer toward broader evaluation only if the lower confidence bound for its task effect exceeds δ, the agency analysis excludes decrement larger than h, and consequential harms are acceptably bounded. For this rule, simulate several true effects above δ and report inconclusive-outcome probabilities; power at δ is not a meaningful success target. Bounds spanning benefit and harm are inconclusive. A task-effect upper bound below δ supports stopping a worthwhile-benefit claim for this setting. A protocol benefit with no visualizer benefit supports the protocol alone. Freeze confidence levels, error control for multiple claims, δ, h, missing-data rules, and any interim looks before recruitment; do not stop early when significance first appears.

## Resources, withdrawal, harms, and records

For a separate all-information block, compare interacting teams with nominal teams containing the same number of independent solvers, identical complete dossiers, devices, training, and person-minutes. Score the best nominal member as a deliberately demanding upper-bound benchmark; its retrospective selection advantage must be acknowledged. Randomize this comparison separately or use fresh matched tasks with prespecified order handling. Beating this benchmark supports task-specific coordination advantage; failing it does not disprove collective capability in other tasks.

Track participant and whole-team losses with timing and voluntary reasons. Permit withdrawal without pressure; never replace members mid-trial and pretend team composition was unchanged. Analyze remaining authorized outcomes by assignment. Distinguish actual deadline failure from outcome unavailable after withdrawal or technical loss. Prespecify team-level missingness assumptions and sensitivity bounds; never impute withdrawal as assent or count only completers as randomized evidence.

Stop an individual session for requested withdrawal, distress, harassment, inaccessible essential controls, or false attributed commitments. Pause the study for a serious privacy incident or reproducible attestation defect; repair and independent review precede restart. There are no real consequential commitments. Compensation should not depend on agreement or task success.

Collect pseudonymous team/participant identifiers, task actions, proposal versions, attributed stances, timestamps, errors, and submitted outputs. Record audio only if essential and separately consented; avoid video and intimate self-disclosures. Explain what teammates can see, restrict researcher access, separate contact records, set a retention/deletion schedule, and disclose withdrawal limits once records are deidentified. Public sharing should default to synthetic traces and aggregate results, not recognizable conversation transcripts.

## Later transfer and boundaries

Only after reproducible task benefits, stable measurement, and acceptable harms, test multiple groups coordinating a shared scarce resource over repeated sessions. Randomize at the **whole federation** level where groups influence one another. Compare structured outward summaries with recoverable internal differences against matched summaries lacking that navigation, while preserving information availability through another route. Introduce an external allocation change and measure internal revision, unauthorized overcommitment, joint delivery, and retained disagreement. This tests bidirectional coordination; it does not establish a universal developmental ladder.

Retain 3, 4, and 5 as later small-group comparisons and 12 as a larger comparison; none is a ceiling. Use four initially for tractable complementary cards, not ontological privilege. Eight aspects and twelve lenses remain optional design hypotheses. Uncertainties include accessibility, generalization beyond temporary groups, affinity, authority arrangements, and long-term stewardship.

**Strongest counterexample:** a skilled coordinator using a simple table achieves better plans, preserves dissent, and costs less; geometry adds confusion. This result must count against the visualizer’s utility while leaving the broader research question open.
