**Scope and status:** 19 September 2026. Reviewed `03_INTEGRATED_RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md`, `05_EXECUTION_PLAN_AND_GATES.md`, `studies/FIRST_SESSION_MATERIALS.md`, `lantern_task.json`, `verify_fixture.py`, and `synthetic_check_result.json`. **Overall judgment:** The synthesis consistently narrows the principal causal claim to the practical effect of offering C in addition to B’s protocol, evidence, and capabilities. It correctly separates task capability from attestation, authority, and representational accuracy. The package is useful for development; it remains explicitly unready for confirmatory human collection. One instruction–rubric mismatch should be fixed before final packaging. The remaining items chiefly specify existing future gates.

## Numbered findings and required dispositions

### 1. High — The first prompt and scored answer disagree

**Blocking now:** yes, for presenting these as internally consistent calibration materials.

Facilitator step 2 asks participants to provide “a feasible plan.” Probe P1 requires identification of **both** physically feasible initial plans. An orchard/compact/long answer follows the instruction but could fail P1. This is a scoring defect independent of interface quality.

**Action:** ask “Identify all physically feasible plans, then identify which current plan is authorized,” or revise P1 to accept any feasible plan and measure exhaustive enumeration in a separate explicit prompt. Freeze the chosen wording in the response form as well as facilitator instructions. Keep physical feasibility and authorization separately scored. S2–S4 prompts should likewise specify whether the target is the currently proposed plan or the set of available plans.

### 2. Medium — The fixture passes, but its proof boundary should remain narrow

**Blocking now:** no; preserve the existing limitations.

I reran the verifier’s functions without invoking its file-writing entry point. All **32** state–plan combinations matched the declared sets; action readiness was true, false, false, true for S1–S4. Independently checking the stated card arithmetic also agrees: the large frame fails orchard time and bridge width; the short battery fails runtime; compact/long works on both open routes. Closure leaves bridge only. The recorded result is reproducible.

This is four fixed authority states, not exhaustive authorization verification. The function hard-codes actors A–D; the reported combination count is also hard-coded. It does not establish arbitrary-roster behavior, malformed-record handling, overlapping membership, or version-change correctness in applications.

**Action:** retain the bounded-fixture claim. Before P1 acceptance, derive roster/counts from fixture data and add missing actor, explicit refusal, stale mandate, unauthorized route, missing proposal, and source-correction cases. A second reader should check the rule independently rather than merely reuse the same Boolean implementation.

### 3. Medium — Preserve the practical-offer estimand in every claim

**Blocking now:** no; wording clarification recommended. **Future prerequisite:** operational B/C specification before allocation.

P3 is appropriately an offer-of-visualizer comparison, with navigation and learning costs included. It cannot identify pure geometric encoding, protocol efficacy, or an effect among willing visualizer users. P0’s phrase “equally usable plain representation” should be a design aspiration, not an assumed fact or inclusion criterion selected after results.

**Action:** freeze which controls B contains, how C opens/closes, whether its plain view remains available, its default screen, and the shared tutorial’s exact sequence. Document that equal tutorial duration may leave different proficiency; that is part of this offered package. Log usage for interpretation while retaining assignment-based estimates. A C benefit supports this package on these tasks; a C null includes failure to adopt it under this onboarding.

### 4. High — Power the lower-bound decision, not a conventional zero-effect test

**Blocking now:** no. **Future prerequisite:** P3 planning and registration.

The proposed success rule requires a task-effect lower bound above δ. A study powered merely to reject zero when the true effect is δ does not meet that goal. The protocol now says simulations must match the rule, but the planning specification should make the distinction explicit.

**Action:** choose justified design alternatives **Δdesign > δ**, simulate the probability of crossing the δ bound, and report inconclusive probabilities across plausible effects and variances. Separately model the chance that participation uncertainty excludes harm larger than h. A study can be well powered for task benefit yet incapable of establishing the participation condition. Define whether the overall success decision requires both bounds jointly and specify the corresponding error-control approach. Pilot treatment differences should not set these targets.

### 5. Medium — Complete the decision table, especially uncertainty about harms

**Blocking now:** no. **Future prerequisite:** before interpreting P3 results.

An interval can exclude harm and still straddle δ; this is inconclusive about worthwhile benefit even though it does not span “worthwhile benefit and meaningful harm.” Likewise, task benefit with an imprecise participation estimate is insufficient for the proposed advance rule. “Consequential attribution harms acceptably bounded” currently lacks an event definition, denominator, follow-up window, and tolerance.

**Action:** freeze exhaustive categories: advance, worthwhile benefit excluded, harm concern, and otherwise inconclusive. Define benefit orientation and the h outcome’s units. If positive participation scores mean better agency, safety requires a lower bound above −h. Report attribution incidents separately, with team/session dependence respected; zero observed events cannot establish zero underlying risk.

### 6. High — Distinguish missing measurements from treatment-caused inability to finish

**Blocking now:** no. **Future prerequisite:** endpoint and intercurrent-event strategy before P3.

The text calls technical loss and withdrawal missingness. Lost logs can be missing measurements, but a C crash that prevents a real task submission can be an intervention failure within a practical-offer estimand. Team dissolution can make the intended four-person endpoint undefined. Averaging only surviving tasks would change the target and favor conditions that selectively lose difficult observations.

**Action:** specify separate handling for recording loss, tool-caused interruption, external interruption, individual withdrawal, group withdrawal, and resumed work. Define the fixed task denominator and what counts as an observed task failure without treating refusal as a moral or attestation failure. State which outcomes remain meaningful after composition changes. Preserve authorized assignment-based data, report losses by condition, and run explicit sensitivity analyses/bounds for unavailable outcomes. “Analyze retained data by assignment” alone does not remove attrition bias.

### 7. Medium — Human calibration still needs a frozen delivery contract

**Blocking now:** no; current non-readiness statement is correct. **Future prerequisite:** P2 administration.

The distributed-information and full-record tasks are correctly distinguished. However, the exact presentation of state events, public versus private facts, answer-recording points, deadlines, and feedback needs freezing. Otherwise facilitators may disclose the solution through the S3 explanation in one session and require retrieval in another. Repeated questions also teach version logic, so improvement across S1–S4 is not an independent treatment effect.

**Action:** create participant-facing event cards and forms, a facilitator script, an information-access matrix, and acceptable-answer examples for P5–P8. Mark S1–S4 as a dependent calibration sequence. Review the food-team transfer item before use; a supplied reference to yesterday’s permission can measure comprehension, but does not alone demonstrate spontaneous analogical transfer.

### 8. Medium — Preserve the stage boundaries and specify future denominators

**Blocking now:** no. **Future prerequisites:** P2/P3 launch as already documented.

The 4–6 interview and 8–12 team envelopes are explicitly logistical starting points, not efficacy sample justifications. The package properly lists absent instruments, task bank, review arrangements, registration, and recruitment. Retain these distinctions when summarizing completion.

**Action:** before P2, designate feasibility and calibration outcomes separately, including whether 90% recoverability is measured over scheduled tasks, sessions, or teams and how aborted sessions enter the denominator. Before P3, freeze the exact capability score, task weights, score range, task distribution, time limits, familiarity policy, participant recruitment pool, and number of independent teams supported by sensitivity analysis. More tasks may improve a team’s measurement precision; they do not supply additional randomized teams. These unresolved quantities justify continued instrument development, not a premature sample-size promise.

**Disposition recommendation:** fix finding 1 now; carry findings 2–8 into named P1/P2/P3 acceptance checks. No inspected evidence supports advancing beyond the package’s stated synthetic-calibration status.
