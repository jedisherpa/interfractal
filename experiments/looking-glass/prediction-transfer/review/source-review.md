# Gate 10 source review: unseen views and transfer

Accessed 2026-09-20 in America/Denver. Collector: GPT-5.6 Luna via read-only inspection of original papers and publisher or author archives. This bounded pass supports preregistering prediction, interpolation, new-setting, and changed-task labels. It does not run Gate 10, supply human data, or predict a result for the Looking Glass instrument. Provenance is in [`sources.json`](sources.json).

## Primary methodological findings

Gick and Holyoak (1983) used experiments in which participants read one or more stories illustrating a problem and solution, then attempted a semantically different but structurally analogous transfer problem. Summarization, a statement of the underlying principle, or a diagram changed the likelihood of transfer ([publisher record and DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0285(83)90002-6); [third-party-hosted original-paper copy](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/1983-gick.pdf)). The study supports treating “new setting” as a separately specified target whose surface context changes while a declared relational structure is retained. It does not show that any new case is transfer, establish universal far transfer, or validate the 5-D world model.

Gentner, Loewenstein, and Thompson (2003) tested analogical encoding in three primary studies with novices learning negotiation strategies. Comparing two cases, rather than studying each independently, improved abstraction and later transfer, including a dynamic negotiation exercise ([publisher or author PDF](https://groups.psych.northwestern.edu/gentner/papers/GentnerLoewensteinThompson03.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.95.2.393)). The methodological implication is to score a changed-task family only when its overlap with the source family is declared in advance: shared relational structure, changed surface details, and changed task demand should be separate fields. Their negotiation materials and comparison supports are unlike the finite geometric benchmark, so no effect size or transfer advantage carries over.

Brod, Greve, Jolles, Theobald, and Galeano-Keiner (2022) compared explicit predictions made before numerical facts with postdictions made after the correct answer was shown. Across two experiments, the prediction-before-feedback condition improved later memory for highly expectancy-violating outcomes relative to the postdiction control ([open primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9722848/); [publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-022-02124-x)). The design is a direct precedent for sealing a response before revealing an unseen view, recording the pre-reveal answer as immutable, and separating reveal from later correction. Its outcome is human memory for numerical facts, not geometric prediction accuracy; it does not establish that precommitment improves Gate10 performance.

## Gate10 design constraints

1. Freeze source/query combinations, labels, independent answer derivations, and the transfer-family definition before implementation or reveal. Define overlap fields before scoring: source family, query relation, coordinate regime, surface context, output schema, and task demand.
2. Keep three categories separate: (a) interpolation or same-family recombination, (b) an unseen setting with the declared task relation preserved, and (c) a changed-task/analogical transfer family with a new surface context and explicitly declared retained structure. Do not collapse these into one “generalization” score.
3. Record a prediction commit event before the answer or view is revealed. The committed response must be immutable; reveal, correction, and any later explanation are separate events. For a future first-exposure assessment, the precommit browser state must not receive a protected explanation or target result before sealing. Software validation may inspect the answer key and therefore verifies event ordering and served-state boundaries only; it does not establish blind human answer construction.
4. Separate practice and same-task reuse from scored first exposure. A familiar source/query combination cannot count as transfer merely because its pixels or labels changed. Report raw correctness and uncertainty by category.
5. Software can verify frozen manifests, independent answer calculations, event ordering, and leakage exclusions. It cannot establish human learning, transfer, calibration, or a benefit of prediction.

The cited experiments motivate these controls but are heterogeneous human studies. Their reported outcomes are not pooled, extrapolated, or treated as a Gate10 effect estimate.
