# Gate 13 source review: a persuasive but wrong view

Accessed 2026-09-20. Collector: GPT-5.6 Luna via `web__run`. This bounded review covers two primary studies relevant to a frozen misleading representation, independent checking evidence, and a disclosed correction/debrief. Their participant results are empirical context only. The Gate 13 software fixture can establish a data/representation mismatch and equal access to the checking record; it cannot establish persuasion, resistance, confidence calibration, or correction success without an approved human study.

## Primary methodological findings

**[Pandey, Manivannan, Nov, Satterthwaite, and Bertini (2014), “The Persuasive Power of Data Visualization,” IEEE Transactions on Visualization and Computer Graphics](https://doi.org/10.1109/TVCG.2014.2346419)** ([author-associated full-paper copy](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5502f56fe4b0aa0a8/t/5da3d77afe2f655194b13312/1571018619600/persuasion-tvcg2014.pdf)). Across randomized crowd-sourced experiments, the authors compared charts with tables in messages designed to change attitudes. They report a conditional pattern: charts were more persuasive than tables for participants without strong initial attitudes, while tables could be more persuasive for participants strongly opposed to the message; they also identify topic, attitude, message content, and medium as entangled factors. For Gate 13, this motivates testing whether a polished view can make a false implication salient while keeping the underlying facts fixed, and recording the initial representation separately from later checking evidence. It does not show that geometric presentation persuades, that a wrong view will fool anyone, or that a visual effect generalizes beyond the selected topics and treatments.

**[Wijnker, Burger, Smeets, and Willems (2026), “Debunking misleading graphs effectively: How vocationally educated young adults perceive graphs,” PLOS One](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0340100)** ([publisher full text](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0340100); preregistration and data links are provided by the article). In a preregistered mixed-method study, the authors used fictional graphs with truncated axes, distorted pictorial areas, and 3D pies, then compared full-design and clean-design corrections. Their survey sample was 130 vocationally educated young adults, with 10 think-aloud interviews. The paper reports that corrections reduced the misleading effect and reduced susceptibility to new misleading graphs of the same type, with no detected difference between clean and full correction designs. For Gate 13, the methodological lesson is to preserve the misleading view, expose an accurate comparator or checking evidence, identify the exact visual/data mismatch, and disclose the correction path. These are participant findings from specific graph types and population; they do not validate the Looking Glass fixture or establish debriefing efficacy here.

## Gate 13 constraints

1. Freeze the underlying facts, the independently derived correct answer, and the deliberately misleading representation before any correction is shown. Keep the visual claim and the data-level answer separately addressable.
2. Make checking evidence available on equal terms: source facts, derivation, mismatch description, and correct representation must be retrievable without changing the underlying fixture or silently rewriting the original view.
3. Log the correction/debrief as a distinct disclosed state. It may explain which encoding or inference was wrong and point to the source evidence; it must not invent a new fact or erase the original misleading artifact.
4. Validate the mismatch and correction in both the geometric/semantic view and an equivalent plain representation. Record software hashes and access paths only as instrument evidence. Do not report susceptibility, persuasion, confidence change, or successful debiasing without actual participants and prespecified outcomes.

## Source limits

Both studies are human empirical research with particular populations, topics, graph forms, and procedures. Pandey et al. explicitly leave the causal mechanism of chart-versus-table differences unresolved; Wijnker et al. report graph-type and sample-specific correction results, including preregistration deviations disclosed in their analysis. Neither supports a human result for this public synthetic fixture. The current gate remains a software-level counterexample and correction-path check.
